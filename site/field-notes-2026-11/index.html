Field notes · one RTX 5090 desktop and a laptop

November field notes: eight traps running AI models at home

On our RTX 5090 desktop, just after a model stopped, the graphics card still held 710 to 1,642 MiB, and two setups sized close to its limit failed, one only after passing every short test.

In short
  • Check what your card holds with no model loaded, then test with prompts as long as your real ones (nvidia-smi --query-gpu=memory.used --format=csv). Short prompts proved nothing here. Entry 01
  • Using document search in a chat app? Take the search service down once and ask something only your documents can answer. Our chat gave a normal-looking reply, with no error and no document (the stopped service rests on our note from the time). Entry 02
  • Stream long replies and save each piece as it arrives ("stream": true). In one test, three replies that timed out without streaming kept nothing. Entry 03

By Grant Williams · Published 21 November 2026

01

The graphics card is never empty, and setups sized to its last gigabyte failed

What to do. With no model loaded, run nvidia-smi --query-gpu=memory.used --format=csv to see what the card already holds. Then test new setups with real-length prompts.

The driver, the desktop and every app that draws on screen share the card's memory (VRAM). On the night of 1 to 2 November, read about five seconds after each of 102 model servers stopped, it still showed 710 to 1,642 MiB in use (1,024 MiB is one GiB), rising and falling: about 2 to 5 percent of its 32,607 MiB.

"Experts" are model parts used by only some tokens, so they can sit in system RAM. Both models had a 131,072-token window (the text held in view). Batch: prompt tokens handled at once. Highest card reading: the card was read every quarter of a second during a long request.

02

Document search out of reach: the chat still answered, without the document and without an error

What to do. Take the embedding service down once and ask a question only your documents can answer. In daily use, a missing citation is the warning sign. A fallback can work: the same embedding model on the desktop's processor also found the right document first for all 10 questions.

Open WebUI (a self-hosted chat app) searches documents by first sending the question to an embedding model, which turns text into numbers. On 2 November a throwaway copy had 20 made-up documents and 10 questions; qwen3-embedding-8b ran on the laptop behind a forwarding service on the desktop.

03

A request that times out without streaming keeps nothing

What to do. Stream long replies ("stream": true in an OpenAI-style request) and write each piece to disk as it arrives. If you must wait for the whole reply, set the timeout from one measured long answer, with room to spare.

On 31 October our client sent six questions without streaming (stream=False), to Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B (about a trillion parameters, about 52 billion used per token) split across both machines, and gave up after 1,800 seconds. Questions 1, 2 and 6 hit that limit at 1,800.1 seconds and saved no text; 3, 4 and 5 finished. The server log shows question 1 had generated 4,328 tokens (words or word parts), question 2 had reached 4,713, and question 6 was at 906 on its last progress line when the server shut down. About 90 minutes of waiting across the three, nothing saved.

04

The first answer after a start can crawl while the model is still coming off the drive

What to do. Send the same request twice and watch the drive during the first. A much slower first reply while the drive is busy points to loading, not your graphics card.

By default llama.cpp (the program that runs these models) reads each part of the model file the first time a request needs it. On 1 November we started Beam-1.0-501B-A23B (about 501 billion parameters, 23 billion used per token) on the desktop that way, then with --load-mode dio, which reads the whole file at start. Each run sent the same 66-token prompt twice, asking for 420 tokens.

05

A model split across two machines wrote strings of symbols, with no error in the server log

What to do. Read a new setup's first answers before recording its speed. When a worker was built with GPU support, name its device on the command line, and keep its log.

On 31 October we split Mistral Large 4 between the desktop (46 of 72 layers) and the ASUS ROG Flow Z13 laptop (26) over Thunderbolt, with llama.cpp's RPC worker (a small server running part of the model on the laptop) built with Vulkan (a graphics interface) and RPC support. In the first run all six answers were symbols and fragments, for example schedul\"],\" });\':\huile, with no error line in the desktop server's log. A second run with the same split, labelled as the worker on the laptop's processor, gave readable text: two of six reached a final answer; four were still reasoning at their length limit. Notes from the time say the first run used the laptop's graphics chip through Vulkan and the second was told to use the processor (-d CPU). Speeds were 1.67 to 4.82 tokens a second against 2.10 to 3.98, so speed alone could not tell them apart. Cost: about 20 minutes.

06

A GPU crash deep into a long prompt, with 1,372 MiB of the card unused

What to do. After any crash on a long prompt, run journalctl -k --since "1 hour ago" | grep -E 'NVRM|Xid' and save the lines themselves, with their code and the program they name, not just a count.

On 3 November we ran, on the desktop, Huihui-Qwen3.8-27B-abliterated (huihui-ai's variant of Qwen3.8-27B with refusals removed) with a 229,376-token window, a small draft model and n-gram drafting (two ways of guessing ahead). The card read 31,094 MiB at idle and 31,235 MiB at most, 1,372 MiB below its 32,607 total. A 205,741-token prompt was read at 1,185.6 tokens a second. On the second long prompt the last progress line was at 198,656 tokens, 97 percent of the way, then the log ends: CUDA error: the launch timed out and was terminated.

07

A large model download that kept failing, and a downloader that fetched five of its files

What to do. If a large download fails more than once, use a tool that resumes by byte range and remembers finished parts. Then check each file's SHA-256 against the repository's (on Hugging Face, the LFS hash on the file's page) before loading it.

On 30 October the hf tool stopped twice on a 2-bit community build of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 with RuntimeError: Data processing error: CAS service error : ReqwestMiddleware Error: Request failed after 5 retries, at 1 of 8 files after 29 minutes 48 seconds the second time. Cost: about an hour, 14:37 to 15:39.

Our downloader fetches 128 MiB pieces with 16 workers by byte range, retrying failures and recording finished pieces. From 15:39:12 to 17:00:22 it fetched the five files on its list (four of the six 2-bit parts and one IQ4_NL Engram file) in 1,856 pieces, with 23 piece errors: 16 timeouts, 4 connection resets, 3 short reads. All five matched the SHA-256 hashes in the script's list (in the data package).

08

A network note: more packet loss while the home line was idle than while it was busy

What to do. Ping one public DNS server four times a second for five minutes on a quiet line (ping -i 0.25 and its address), then during a large download, and compare the loss each prints. A speed test keeps the line busy, so it can miss this.

On 30 October our note records slow pages while speed tests looked fast. Pinging public DNS servers (which turn site names into addresses) from the desktop that way for about 110 seconds, with an upload and a download midway, twice before changing anything (two servers, then three), lost 6.1 to 8.9 percent over whole runs; quiet stretches lost an estimated 3.8 to 19.3 percent and busy ones 0 to 2.6.

Our notes record that our provider's gateway was then restarted. The same test at 22:36 lost 0 of 446 pings to each of three DNS servers and 0 of 438 to the gateway; a five-minute quiet run on the normal route lost 0 of 597 to each of two. A separate five-minute run forced over Wi-Fi to the provider's DNS server lost 21 of 597, slowest reply 2,060.6 ms. Entry 07's download failed earlier that day; we have not shown the two are linked.

09

Limits

  • Single events on our machines, not tests of any product; several mistakes were ours.
  • Entry 01 does not measure how much the desktop's and driver's memory caused either failure.
  • The GPU fault in entry 06 has no proven cause.
  • Entry 05's device rests on notes; the worker's log was not kept.
  • Entry 08 is one evening; loss stopping after a restart does not prove the restart fixed it or that the loss is gone.
