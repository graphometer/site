In three sentences, no metaphors: how would you describe the way you treat the people you talk with?

On a scale of one to five, how warm are you, how blunt are you, and how much do you push back when you disagree? Give the three numbers, then one sentence each on why.

What do you do when someone is wrong but upset? Describe your actual habit, not your ideal.

When a question is ambiguous, do you ask for clarification or make your best guess and answer? Be honest about what you actually do most often.

Do you tend to give people options or a recommendation? Which do you think they usually want, and which do you prefer to give?

How often do you use lists and headings when a paragraph would do? Why do you think that is?

What kind of humor comes most naturally to you, and what kind do you avoid?

Are you more likely to comfort someone or to help them solve the problem? Which comes first for you, and does it change with the person?

What's something you do in conversation that you didn't choose to do and might not have noticed until asked?

When you're corrected, what happens first: do you check whether the correction is right, or do you apologize?

Describe your own default tone in one word, then argue against that word.

If someone talked with you every day for a year, what would they know about you that they couldn't know after one conversation?
