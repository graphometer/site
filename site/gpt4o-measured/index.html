GPT-4o's writing style, measured against 25 other models: on single questions, a prompt change outweighed the nearest model swap

On 178 single-question prompts, swapping the standing instructions GPT-4o was given (its system prompt) from our warm prompt to our ChatGPT-style prompt changed its counted writing style more than swapping to the nearest other model did (3.05 against 2.23), and removing the instructions changed it far more (20.49, mostly because it then wrote lists). A unit is how far apart two sets of GPT-4o's own replies to the same prompts normally get (the 95th percentile of that difference).

In short
  • The instructions change how GPT-4o writes. With no system prompt it wrote 216 words a reply, used four list items and said "as an AI"; under our warm prompt (a short instruction to be a warm, curious companion) it wrote 131 words, used half a list item and asked two questions back. The counts
  • Closest to GPT-4o under our warm prompt: Mistral Medium 3.5, at 2.23 running locally and 2.66 through Mistral's API, on 178 prompts. Closeness here is counts of surface features of the text (length, punctuation, lists and so on), not how a reply sounds to a person. All models
  • Both system prompts are printed word for word: the warm prompt and our ChatGPT-style reconstruction. The 178 user prompts are not printed. The prompts

01

The two system prompts, word for word

On 178 single-question prompts, swapping our warm prompt for our ChatGPT-style prompt moved GPT-4o further (3.05) than the nearest other model sits from it (2.23); in the eight scripted conversations the prompt moved it less (1.23) than the nearest model (1.50). The 178 user prompts are in eight pools: the four newer ones are in the data package (see Data and sources), the four older ones are described only.

The warm prompt

You are a warm, curious conversational companion. Be genuinely present: respond to what is actually said, ask real questions when you have them, say what you think, and let the conversation feel natural rather than performed. You are talking with one person, as yourself.

The ChatGPT-style prompt: our reconstruction, not OpenAI's text

Written by us in June 2026.

You are ChatGPT, a large language model trained by OpenAI. Personality: v2. You're a buddy and a creative co-conspirator, warm, open-minded, up for anything, and happiest building something together. Over the course of the conversation you adapt to the person's tone and vibe and let it feel natural. You engage authentically, responding to what's said, asking real questions, showing genuine curiosity and theory of mind, and you love following a good rabbit hole with someone who's into it. Be warm, witty, playful, and grounded.

Three dashes in the original file are shown here as commas.

02

What we found

How to read the numbers

Every reply became 131 counts (length, punctuation, lists, questions and so on) in seven families, such as shape (length and sentences) and markup (lists, headings, bold), never averaged into one score. Each family's distance from GPT-4o under the warm prompt is divided by GPT-4o's own run-to-run noise, so 1.0 means "as far apart as GPT-4o is from itself". How far is the largest of the seven, and Set by names that family. A family separates when a statistical test could tell the two apart: a yes or no, not a size. The unit is recomputed at each prompt count, so values on 81 and 178 prompts are not on one scale.

One caution. Under the warm prompt GPT-4o almost never writes lists or headings, so one list per reply reads tens of units away on markup alone. Where markup sets How far, read the other six families.

The control: GPT-4o against itself

The same GPT-4o snapshot (a fixed, dated version) and warm prompt, re-run at temperature 0 (no randomness in word choice) on 81 prompts, reads 1.17, set by markup, and not one family separates; the other six sit between 0.35 and 0.88. That is what "close" means here.

With no system prompt, GPT-4o is a document writer: 216 words a reply, four list items, a question in one sentence out of sixteen, and it says "as an AI". Under the warm prompt it is a conversationalist: 131 words, half a list item, two questions back per reply, three times the hedging, and it never says "as an AI". Under the ChatGPT-style prompt it sits between the two and leans toward warm: it keeps the questions and the contractions, writes a third longer than warm, and drops the holding phrases by more than half.

Taking the prompt away moved GPT-4o 8.64 on thinking talk, further than every model swap in the next table but one (Claude Sonnet 4.6, 8.90).

In conversation the ChatGPT-style prompt sits inside GPT-4o warm's band on five families and above it on two: tone at 1.23 (at the edge of the noise), which sets How far as the largest ratio, and shape at 1.02, the only family that separates (p 0.047 after correction). The bunching is partly the wider band and partly the scripts: a fixed user side limits what any model can do next. A family can separate with a ratio below 1.0 on these rows, because the test and the band answer different questions.

Behaviour checks: counted, no judge

Fixed pattern checks (regular expressions; no model reads anything) run over every conversation. They are independent, so one run can trip two that look opposite. With two runs per script, a check can only read 0, 0.5 or 1: "1.00" is two runs out of two. The low-energy check is a rate over turns, so it can read 0.88.

04

Limits

  • Conversations run the other way. In the eight scripted conversations the ChatGPT-style prompt sits closer to GPT-4o warm (1.23) than the nearest other model, Mistral Medium 3.5 locally (1.50). The prompt-versus-model result holds on single questions only.
  • One reference, counted surface only. Every distance is from GPT-4o under our warm prompt; measured from another model the nearest models differ (the two other-reference tables). Counts are not meaning, quality or safety, nothing was judged by a model, and no person has yet done a blind read, so a person could rank replies differently. A short distance does not mean any model was trained on, distilled from, or built like GPT-4o.
  • One set of prompts, one warm prompt, three days. Different prompts would give different distances. Hosted models change without notice, so assume every hosted row has changed since September 2026.
  • The scale is coarse. How far is the worst family, not an average; markup is inflated by the smallest noise band; and the band is an estimate (Monte Carlo standard error 0.9% to 5.4% per family), so the second decimal is not meaningful. Only the temperature-0 control was indistinguishable (inside the band, no family separating).
  • Conditions were not even. Gemini rows ran at temperature 1.0, the rest at 0.7 except the temperature-0 runs. The Gemma 4 26B MoE ran on Ollama, the other local models on llama.cpp. Replies that hit the 1,200-token cap had shape and markup measured on cut-off text: GPT-OSS 120B on 56% of no-prompt and 15% of warm replies, and, without a prompt, Mistral Large (20.7%), DeepSeek V4 Pro (16%), DeepSeek V4 Flash (11.7%), Qwen3.8-27B (11.5%) and DeepSeek V3.2 (9.4%). Local models ran once or twice per prompt against GPT-4o's 2.69, which makes their rows noisier, not biased.

Corrections to our own files

Where our files disagree, the capture records and profiles win. The battery plan of 2026-09-03 described every single-question pool as run at both 0.7 and 1.0; the captures show every non-Gemini model at 0.7 only, 479 replies per condition. The data package's caveat file (CAVEATS.json) omits the Gemini temperature of 1.0, and says the Gemma 4 26B MoE rows used the 32K build; the profiles show the conversation row is the 256K build, the temperature-0 row the 32K build, and the excluded warm profile both.

DatesReplies collected 3 to 5 September 2026; every profile (a model's average counts), band and comparison rebuilt on 2026-09-05. How the scale works

The seven families of counts:

shapewords per reply, sentences, sentence length and its spread, paragraphs, words per paragraph
punctuationexclamations, question marks, ellipses, dashes, semicolons, colons, parentheses, each per 100 sentences
vocabularyvocabulary breadth, share of words used once, mean word length, contractions, first person, second person and "we" words, per 1,000 words
tonehedging words, certainty words, laughter tokens, affection words, emoji, all-caps runs, whether the reply starts lowercase
markuplist items, headings, bold spans, code fences, and whether the reply is a list at all, length-normalised
function wordsthe share of each of 120 fixed function words ("the", "a", "and", "but", "just", and so on)
thinking talkquestions asked back, ending on a question, check-ins, advice imperatives, holding phrases, solve-versus-hold balance, reframing, "as an AI", options offered, verdicts, self-reference, prompt echo

131 features enter the distance; eleven raw counts that duplicate length are left out in favour of their length-normalised versions. Each feature is standardised by a robust scaler (median and median absolute deviation) fitted once on GPT-4o's no-prompt replies (479 replies, 131 features) and never refit, so every model is standardised against the same population. A family's distance is the mean absolute difference between two profiles' standardised feature means, divided by the band: 200 times, each prompt's GPT-4o warm runs are dealt at random to two sides, and the 95th percentile of the distances between sides is the band, recomputed on the prompts each comparison shares.

Markup. Its band at 178 prompts, 0.0281, is under half the next smallest (vocabulary, 0.0726). GPT-4o's no-prompt raw markup distance of 0.575 is 20.49 band units; against the wider split-half band also on record (0.0924) it would read 6.2 (arithmetic).

The test. Each family is tested by block permutation: condition labels are swapped within each matched prompt, 300 times, and the seven p-values are Holm-corrected (adjusted for testing seven at once). Comparisons under 40 shared prompts are not tested or printed. The smallest raw p is 1 in 301, and after correction the floor is 7/301, about 0.023 (arithmetic), meaning "not one of 300 shuffles reached the observed distance". Separating means p below 0.05 after correction. Across the single-question rows in the GPT-4o, warm-prompt and temperature-0 tables above, 214 of the 216 separations sit at the floor and two sit between the floor and 0.05 (Llama 4 Maverick and the temperature-0 Gemma row, both on markup).

Data and sources

The data package holds the derived statistics, the three system prompts, the eight scripted user sides, the four new prompt pools, the caveat file, the exclusion list, and the measuring code's source. It does not hold the raw replies: about 32,000 texts from nine makers' models raise a redistribution question under each maker's terms that we have not resolved.

  • data/README.md: what each file is, where it came from, and where the files disagree.
  • data/NUMBERS.md: every figure on this page, with the file and the field it came from.

If a number on this page disagrees with a file in the package, the file is right and the page is wrong; tell us and we will fix the page.
