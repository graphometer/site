Measured study · one RTX 5090 desktop · 19 models

Two llama.cpp settings for MoE models bigger than your GPU

On one RTX 5090, loading weights into RAM at start, not from the drive as needed, made long prompts read 2.0 to 3.1 times faster on 12 large models, costing free RAM. Expert-cache setups, most with other changes, wrote short replies 1.4 to 2.9 times faster on 11 of 16 large models; the other 5 had no cache.

In short
  • If your model file is memory-mapped (read from the drive as needed), read its weights into RAM once at start (--load-mode dio). Long prompts read faster, cold starts were shorter, and writing speed stayed within a few percent. The cost is free RAM: one model left as little as 5.1 GiB of 188 GiB free (8.8 GiB in its hand-started run). What to do
  • For faster writing, try an expert cache on the card (--moe-cache-mib). Setups that added it wrote short replies 1.44 to 2.88 times faster on 11 large models, but all of them also used the first setting, most changed other settings too, and the text written changed on 13 of 15 cached setups. Results
  • Size the cache to the card, and use a build that has it. Eleven cache setups ran out of card memory. Both flags are in llama.cpp release b12015. A command to start from

By Grant Williams · Published 21 November 2026

01

What to do

A mixture-of-experts (MoE) model keeps most of its learned weights in many small parts called experts, and uses a few for each token (a word or part of one). llama.cpp can keep experts in system RAM (--cpu-moe for all, --n-cpu-moe N for N layers' worth, --fit on to decide from free card memory); every model here kept some or all of its experts there. Reading is how fast a prompt goes in, writing how fast the reply comes out, in tokens a second (t/s). GiB and MiB are close to a gigabyte and a megabyte.

  1. Read the weights in once at start (--load-mode dio). By default llama.cpp memory-maps the file: the system pulls pieces from the drive as they are first used into its page cache, spare RAM it can take back. dio (direct I/O) reads them once into pinned memory, RAM locked in place, which counts as used. If the weights are already read into RAM (--no-mmap or --load-mode none), expect only a shorter load.
  2. Setups that added the expert cache wrote faster (--moe-cache-mib N), though most of them changed other settings too (results). The cache sets aside N MiB of card memory for copies of the most-used experts. An expert in the cache (a hit) is computed on the card; a miss is uploaded. Its documentation (docs/moe-cache.md in the pull request 31286 source) says it is used for small batches, such as writing, and that long prompts still upload the experts they use. 18000 is a little under 18 GiB.
  3. On these models the cache was paired with --cpu-moe, usually with --fit off, which turns off the automatic placement. All experts then sit in RAM, and the cache decides which of them also live on the card.
  4. Make the cache as big as fits, with room to spare. Too small could be slower than none (sizing).
  5. Use a build with both flags, such as release b12015.
  6. Compare output only with the same placement: under --fit on, two starts can write different text (output checks).

A command to start from

llama-server -m MODEL.gguf --n-gpu-layers 99 --cpu-moe --fit off \
  --load-mode dio --moe-cache-mib 18000 \
  --ctx-size 262144 --batch-size 4096 --ubatch-size 2048 \
  --threads 8 --threads-batch 8

The cached Qwen3-Coder-Next command as it ran, shortened to the flags that matter here (sampling and chat-template flags left out; the full command lines for every run are in the data package). Before, it kept 28 layers' experts in RAM (--n-cpu-moe 28).

  • Qwen3.5-122B-A10B: 12,000 MiB (b12015, all experts in RAM) wrote prose faster (41.49 t/s against 32.45), code slower (40.54 against 42.79), read about the same (498.2 against 518.2) and needed a newer build. 17,000 MiB ran out of card memory.

Other checks on the after setups

  • Code words in long reads: 3 of 3 came back on the 14 large models tested on 1 November, at about 2,000 and 48,000 tokens and at 97,000 or 200,000, and on all 16 large after setups at 48,000. (GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and MiMo-V2.6-Flash ran the long test with a cache 1,000 MiB larger than their final one.)
  • Questions and tools: on the 14 long-read runs, 5 of 5 tool round trips (the model calls a tool and uses the result) on all, 5 of 5 short questions on all but Inkling-Small (4 of 5; its answer “does not contain” was right, but the automatic check did not accept it).
  • Prompts at the batch edges: 24 prompts, 19 sized just under, at and over the batch boundaries and 5 of random size; all 24 got a reply with no server error on all 15 cached setups. This shows the server answers, not what it answers.

Limits

  • One machine. How fast experts cross from RAM to the card (the PCIe connection) decides much of the cache's value and was not measured. Another connection, less RAM or another card will move every number.
  • These files. One quantization per model, at the windows shown. Another file changes expert size and what fits.
  • Single runs, one request at a time. One start can differ from the next (Beam 1.0's stall). Nothing here measures two users or batched requests.
  • Mixed changes. Thirteen cached rows also moved experts into RAM, seven rows (six models) changed build, four the micro-batch and one the conversation cache.
  • A quiet desktop, narrow quality checks. No other large program ran in RAM or on the card; on setups that left 5.1 to 21.1 GiB free, what happens when one does was not tested. No broad or human-judged quality comparison was run.

How this was measured

  • Machine: the desktop on Two boxes: RTX 5090 (32 GB), 188 GiB RAM, Intel Core Ultra 9 285K; 8 threads on cores 0 to 7; one request, one model at a time.
  • Dates: 1 November 2026 afternoon into early 2 November (16 models); 2 November morning (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3-30B-A3B) and afternoon (Ornith-1.5-35B).
  • Before: each model's usual command on 1 November, started by hand from a cold page cache. After: through a test copy of its start script.
  • Speed test: two prose answers to a 22 to 162 token prompt, capped at 420 tokens; a code rewrite of a 9,000-character Python file, capped at 700; a read of about 20,000 tokens of llama.cpp source; a next turn capped at 300. Temperature 0.15 (0.2 for the read), seed 42; server timings, counting reasoning tokens.
  • Card and RAM: nvidia-smi and Linux MemAvailable, every 2 seconds, over the after test and its checks. Two repeat starts of Beam 1.0 peaked at 31,402 and 31,409 MiB.
  • Builds: release b12015 and installed builds that had the cache (commits 23b0202, 10b8b75, 184f653). Flags read in tools/server/README.md of b12015 and 23b0202. The pull request 31286 build reports build 12057; the cache's documentation is its docs/moe-cache.md.
  • Figures: ratios and percentages come from two measured figures; the data package says which two.
