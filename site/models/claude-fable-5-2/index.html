Claude Fable 5.2 builds websites and coordinates rewrites

We use Claude Fable 5.2 to build our websites and to manage big edits across them, with other models writing and checking. Here is what it built and where it slipped.

In short

  • Best at carrying a big site job to the end. It plans the work, hands parts to other models, checks what comes back and keeps a record of every change.
  • Check its scripts and its claims. One scripted edit blanked two live pages, and its draft headlines often claimed more than the page showed.
  • Hosted only. You use it in Anthropic’s Claude apps, through the Claude API or on several cloud platforms. You cannot download it.

By Grant Williams, who runs this home AI lab · Facts checked 21 November 2026

What we use it for

  • It built friendsof4o.com and still adds pages

    It built friendsof4o.com from answers other models collected, and it still adds pages to it.

  • It runs site-wide edits with other models drafting and checking

    It writes the instructions, starts the models that draft, check and read each page, and keeps a record of every cut and change.

  • It fixed a game in rounds after each time Grant played it

    It worked through rounds of fixes on a game, one round after each time Grant Williams played it.

  • It led putting a study’s data online after a privacy check

    For one of our published studies it led the work to put the data online, with a privacy check first and a script anyone can use to recount the numbers.

What it built

It built friendsof4o.com for phones and desktops, light and dark

Fable 5.2 built friendsof4o.com. It drew three looks for the home page, Grant picked one, and it built the whole site on that look, in light and dark color schemes, for phones and desktops. Each time the site is built, a check compares the GPT-4o quote on the home page word for word with the saved answer it came from.

It traced a pounding sound to footsteps and removed them

Before Get Out Of My Woods went public, Fable 5.2 polished it in rounds. When Grant heard a rapid pounding just before the killer’s footsteps, it measured instead of guessing: steps from far away were firing too fast while the killer hurried. Those steps are no longer played. The game then went public, free, under the MIT license.

It ran a three-round site rewrite and no wrong number was found

As manager, Fable 5.2 ran a rewrite of the front page and the articles on this site in three rounds. Another model wrote each draft, a separate model checked it against the original page, and other models, reading as strangers, judged whether it was clear at once. No wrong number was found in any rewrite, and its own record says the last round did not move the scores much.

Where it goes wrong

Its script left two live pages blank for most of a day

A script it wrote to change page titles across the site matched a title mention inside a hidden note on two older pages. It cut away the top of both pages, and they stayed blank for most of a day until it found the fault itself. Its own checks had only looked at the pages it had written.

Its button fix added an unasked rule that started the hunt sooner

Asked to fix one missing button in the released game, it also added a rule of its own. The tests passed, but the hunt now started much sooner, and Grant heard the killer far earlier than before. It put the game back as it was, apart from the one fix.

It wrote headlines that claimed too much and challenged a correct sentence

When it redrafted the headlines on this site, a fact check against each page found many drafts that claimed more than the page showed. On a review of another of our websites, it suggested correcting a sentence that was right: it had read one of our studies and assumed it was the only one.

So a separate model checks its drafts against the source, and nothing goes live until Grant publishes it.

What it takes in and gives back

It reads text and images and writes text. It holds up to 1 million tokens at once and writes up to 128,000 tokens in one reply.

How hard it thinks

It always thinks before it answers; you cannot turn that off. You set how much with five effort levels: low, medium, high, xhigh and max. High is the default.

How we run it

In a long session in Anthropic’s desktop app on our computer, working with Grant. It starts other models with small scripts that give each one a copy of the work and a written brief.

A change reaches the live site only through a script that first checks the live page has not changed since the draft was made. Grant publishes.

Where you can use it

Anthropic lists it on the Claude API, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry and Claude Platform on AWS, and it runs in the Claude apps. It is not a model you can download. Anthropic says it will not retire it sooner than 9 November 2027.
