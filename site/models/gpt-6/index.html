GPT-6 models build games and check drafts

Astra is OpenAI’s model for demanding work and Sol is its cheaper everyday one. They write parts of our games and read our work before it goes out. Each example names its version; the newest is 6.2.

In short

  • Best at careful, line by line checking. They read the records behind a page or the code behind a game and point to the exact line that is wrong.
  • Astra repeated a mistake from out-of-date files. When the files Astra was handed were out of date, it repeated their mistake, and what these models write still needs a checker.
  • Hosted only. OpenAI offers them in ChatGPT Work, in its coding agent Codex (a program in which it works on its own, step by step) and through its API. You cannot download them.

By Grant Williams, who runs Graphometer · Facts checked 21 November 2026

What we use it for

  • Astra writes single parts of a game, like sound or art

    We give a version of Astra one part of a game, such as the sound or the creature art, while another session puts the parts together and tests them.

  • Sol checked new articles against the records behind them

    In November, GPT-6.2 Sol read new articles for this site against the records behind them, looking for claims the evidence does not support. GPT-6.2 Astra read one of them.

  • Astra reviews plans, and two names it suggested are in use

    We give it a plan and the files behind it and ask what is missing. GPT-6.2 Astra reviewed the plan for friendsof4o.com; its name for the guessing game, “Guess which answer came from GPT-4o”, was that page’s title for a time. GPT-6 Astra suggested “End here”, now one of the five labels in our Five Moves, a short paragraph that lets a chat AI say no.

  • Astra wrote one of our studies, then another model checked it

    GPT-6 Astra wrote our study on estimating a big model’s speed from bytes per token. Another model then read it for faults.

  • Astra searches the web alongside models from other makers

    We give two or three models from different makers the same question and join what they find. GPT-6 Astra has been one of them, with web search turned on.

What it did well

Sol caught an unsupported claim and leaked addresses before publishing

GPT-6.2 Sol read a draft of our article on running Letta Code locally against the program traces behind it. It showed that what the draft called opened secure connections were only attempts, with no sign they finished. It also found that the data files meant to go out with the page still held real network addresses and other details from our machine. All of it was fixed or dropped before the page went live.

Three Astra sessions built a shark game’s sound, shark and chase

Grant escaped the shark too easily in an early build of Great White, an underwater survival game. Three GPT-6 Astra sessions then took the sound, the shark’s look and movement, and the chase, while a lead session put the game together and tested it. Their work added underwater sound and three skill levels.

Astra traced a game’s early start to one exact line

After a fix to Get Out of My Woods, Grant heard the killer far sooner than before. GPT-6 Astra traced it to the exact line: the first search of a cabin ended the game’s calm opening. Reviewing the correction, it also found a scene that told the player to find the keys and battery even when they already had both. We fixed that in the same update.

Where it goes wrong

Astra repeated a wrong label from the project’s notes

Reviewing the friendsof4o.com plan, GPT-6.2 Astra said one Mistral model was a hosted model, not an open one, because the project’s notes listed it that way. Grant corrected it: the model has open weights, and he had run it at home.

Middle of the pack, and it filed away one unfinished row

In our study of one document job, GPT-6.1 Sol landed in the middle of the models we tried. It filed away as finished one row that still had open work, and left one open item that could not be traced back.

A checker found several faults in its study, including an internal word

When GPT-6 Astra wrote a study for this site, a model from another maker found several medium faults, including an internal word left in a code comment meant for publication. Its fixes then drew new findings before the page was ready.

So a model from a different maker checks what it writes, and its reviews get checked against the records before we act on them.

Grant’s note

“The GPT models are better at bounded tasks. I do use them to run autonomous headless CLI sessions, but I’m less likely to have a GPT run an overnight fine-tune or a model test.”

Grant Williams, who runs this lab

These are OpenAI’s standard API prices for prompts of up to 272,000 tokens. Longer prompts cost more, and jobs sent in batches cost less. A token is a word or part of one. Subscription plans are priced separately.

Which one to pick

OpenAI says to use Astra for demanding reasoning and coding tasks, and Sol for everyday coding and agent work. Sol is the cheaper of the two.

What it takes in and gives back

Both read text and images and write text. Each holds up to 1,050,000 tokens at once and writes up to 128,000 tokens in one reply. OpenAI gives Sol’s knowledge cutoff as 1 June 2026 and Astra’s as 18 May 2026.

How we run it

Through Codex, OpenAI’s coding agent, run unattended from the command line on a ChatGPT plan. We set how hard it thinks on every run, usually high or extra high, because an earlier Sol model’s own default in Codex was low.

For reviews it reads a copy of the work, never the live files.

Where you can use it

OpenAI lists both on its API, through the Responses, Chat Completions and Batch endpoints, and in the Codex app and command line. It says both are also in ChatGPT Work on supported plans. Neither is a model you can download.
