Inkling 2 Mini left most test answers blank

Across eight puzzles on one machine, Inkling 2 Mini finished just 24 schedules in 96 tries. Of those schedules, 23 earned the highest possible score.

In short

  • More room let it finish more schedules. The limit covers both thinking and answering. We measured it in tokens, the small pieces of text the model uses. At 512 tokens it finished one schedule in 32 tries. At 2,048 it finished eight in 32. We added the 6,144-token limit after the first two tests left most answers blank. At that limit it finished 15 in 32.
  • Even the largest allowance left 17 of 32 answers blank. In each blank reply the model used its whole allowance thinking and wrote no final answer. The middle wait across all 32 tries at that limit was 58.4 seconds on our machine. For the 14 tries that earned the top score it was 11.6 seconds.
  • One complete schedule scored below the best. On a five-job puzzle the model returned a schedule that followed the rules and earned 25 points. The best possible score was 35.

By Grant Williams, who runs Graphometer · Facts checked 27 November 2026

What our tests showed

More room helped on repeat tries at the same puzzle

We matched tries across limits by using the same puzzle and the same starting number for the model’s random choices. Going from 512 to 2,048 tokens brought seven tries up to the best possible score. Going from 2,048 to 6,144 brought six up to that mark. Every try that earned the top score at a smaller limit earned it again at the next limit.

It found top-scoring schedules from pictures

On the four-job picture puzzle it earned the top score in all four tries at 2,048 tokens and all four at 6,144. On the six-job picture puzzle it reached the top score in two of four tries at 6,144. Each puzzle came in only one format. The test cannot tell us how the same puzzle would fare as a picture and as typed text. The third picture puzzle, the nine-job one, came back blank in all twelve tries, as every puzzle that size did.

What to watch for

Every answer was blank on the three largest puzzles

The puzzles with eight or more jobs accounted for 36 tries. In all 36 the model used its whole allowance thinking and left the final answer empty.

Check the whole answer before using a schedule

Even when the request succeeds, have your program check for text in the final answer. Then check that it can read the whole answer as one complete schedule. At 512 tokens one reply contained text but stopped as it began the entry for job D.

We did not test writing quality, follow-up questions or speed on other machines.

The facts

What did we run?

Each of the eight puzzles gave Inkling 2 Mini a list of jobs to schedule for points. Each try began a fresh conversation with one question. We supplied no separate instructions and gave the model no tools. We tried each puzzle four times at each limit. Each of the four tries used a different starting number for the model’s random choices.

ItemWhat we used
ModelUnsloth’s compressed copy of Inkling 2 Mini is called UD-Q4_K_XL. The model file was 17.6 GB. The separate file for reading pictures was 639 MB.
Graphics cardThe whole model ran on one RTX 5090. It used about 19.6 GB of card memory.
SoftwareWe used version b12816 of llama.cpp, the program that ran the model. We used the files from the Mainline_Support folder.

Unsloth says those files work with llama.cpp from version b12611 onward. It warns that older versions give much worse answers with those files. We tested only b12816.

How much room did the model have?

The question and reply together could fill 8,192 tokens, the small pieces of text the model reads and writes. The longest question used 435 tokens. The reply limit counted both the hidden thinking and the final answer.

We used the maker’s settings for how the model chooses the next piece of text.

How did we score a schedule?

We counted a schedule as complete when our program could read the whole final answer as one schedule in the requested format. To find the best possible score for each puzzle, we checked every combination in advance. Our results matched the scores published with all eight puzzles. A complete schedule earned a place in the best column only if it reached that puzzle’s highest possible score.
