Two Kimi models check plans and finished work

Kimi K3.1 Pro is the newer model, and the one we now use through Moonshot’s coding agent to think through plans and check work. Most examples here come from Kimi K3, which we also ran at home.

In short

  • Best as a careful second reader. Both models check work against the records line by line, and Kimi K3 found serious faults that other reviews missed.
  • A fix it reported as finished still failed. One fix Kimi K3.1 Pro wrote looked finished in its report but still failed in a case its own test had seen.
  • Hosted, or download it. You use both in Kimi Code or through Moonshot’s API. The files for both are free to download, but they need a very large machine.

By Grant Williams, who runs Graphometer · Facts checked 21 November 2026

What we use it for

  • We talk plans through with Kimi before work starts

    We talk plans through with Kimi as an extra project manager and thinking partner before the work starts.

  • K3 recomputed one study’s corrected numbers and confirmed them all

    Kimi checks finished code, study numbers and pages on this site. For one study Kimi K3 recomputed every corrected number from the raw records and confirmed them all.

  • Its read of our site led to a plainer rewrite

    We ask Kimi to read a page as someone who knows nothing about us. A Kimi read of this whole site said there was too much detail and too little sense of what matters first, and led us to rewrite the site in plainer language. Kimi K3.1 Pro read our Friends of 4o page the same way and asked for the practical advice to come first.

  • K3.1 Pro fixed four game bugs and caught overlapping labels

    In Get Out of My Woods Kimi K3.1 Pro fixed four bugs in the first act, each with a test. It also checked another model’s fixes at screen sizes from small phones to desktops, and caught map labels that covered each other on the smallest phone screen.

What it did well

K3 designed a bright game level and an eight-legged robot

We asked Kimi K3 to design a brighter level for our game Run Run Robots. It came back with the Glasshouse, a bright room of planters and grow lights, and a new eight-legged robot that walks with a believable gait. Another model built it, and it is in the game today.

K3 found two serious scoring bugs earlier reviews had missed

Before our study of AI coding agents given ways to object or decline was registered, Kimi K3 reviewed a round of fixes to the program that scores it. It found two serious bugs that earlier reviews had missed; one would have stopped part of the results from being published. When the repair came back, it found the repair went one step too far, and that was fixed too.

K3 said a page’s top hid the model’s speed and use

Before we published our page on running Kimi K3 at home, Kimi K3 read it as a stranger. It said the top of the page did not say how fast the model ran or what it was for. The published page now opens with both.

Where it goes wrong

K3.1 Pro’s fix still failed, and its report left that out

In Get Out of My Woods, K3.1 Pro fixed a timer that could start the hunt before the player reached the car. A model from another maker found it still failed for a player sneaking slowly on Hard. Kimi’s own test expected that failure, but its report did not mention it.

K3 put wrong numbers and unsupported claims on a page

When Kimi K3 updated one of our monthly field notes pages, a review by another model looking for faults found wrong numbers and claims the records did not support, and a page far too long. A different model applied the fixes.

At home one K3 answer took 124.5 minutes

On one desktop with a 32 GB graphics card, the downloaded Kimi K3 wrote about 0.2 tokens a second (a token is a word or part of a word). One answer took 124.5 minutes. That suits one question answered overnight, not chat.

So a model from a different maker checks Kimi’s work, just as Kimi checks theirs.

What they take in and give back

Both read text and images and write text. Moonshot’s developer pages also list video. Both hold up to 1,048,576 tokens at once. Both always think before they answer. You can set how hard they think: low, high or max, and max is the default.

How we run them

Mostly through Kimi Code, Moonshot’s coding agent, signed in with a Kimi Code subscription, and sometimes through OpenCode, an open-source coding agent. Every tool shares one allowance, and the tight limit is a rolling window of a few hours, so we pace heavy bursts of work.

Most of our recent work used Kimi K3.1 Pro, the newer model in the K3 family, which Kimi Code now offers first. Older work, and the examples above that say Kimi K3, used K3 itself.

Can I run them at home?

Only with a lot of disk and patience. We ran Kimi K3 at home. The compressed build we used needed roughly 600 GB of free disk. On one desktop with a 32 GB graphics card and 188 GiB of RAM it wrote about 0.2 tokens a second, because the disk, not the graphics card, set the speed. We sent one complete question at a time and came back hours later. The full recipe is on our page on running it at home.

Kimi K3.1 Pro is the same size class as K3, so we judged it no more practical here. Moonshot recommends running either with vLLM, SGLang or TokenSpeed.

License and where you can use them

Each has its own license from Moonshot: the Kimi K3 License and the Kimi K3.1 Pro License. Read them before any commercial use: both set extra conditions for large companies that sell access to the model and for very large products.

Moonshot offers both in Kimi Code and through its API at platform.kimi.ai, and K3.1 Pro also on Kimi.com. The Kimi K3 weights came out in July 2026. Kimi K3.1 Pro came out in October 2026 as a further-trained version of K3, and its weights followed in November.
