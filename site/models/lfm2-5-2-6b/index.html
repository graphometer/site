LFM2.5-2.6B handles simple steps but fails long documents

LFM2.5-2.6B is a small model from Liquid AI. On one graphics card it handled tool calls well and fit its longest input in little memory, but small reply limits gave empty answers.

In short

  • It passed 84 of 90 simple tool-use runs. It passed 84 of 90 scripted tool-use runs, and no miss was put down to the model.
  • Short reply limits gave empty answers. Its hidden reasoning comes out of the reply budget; give short answers at least 1,024 tokens (a token is a word or part of a word), or they can come back empty with no error.
  • Small, and free for most. Liquid AI publishes official compressed files under its own LFM Open License v1.0, which leaves out commercial use by large companies. The one we used is about 1.7 GB.

By Grant Williams, who runs this home AI lab · Facts checked 21 November 2026

What our tests showed

It passed the steps of 84 of 90 simple tool tests

It passed 84 of 90 runs of 15 scripted tool-use scenarios. All six misses came from one scenario testing a known fault in llama.cpp, the program that runs it. A pass checks the steps, not the quality of the answer.

Found 15 of 15 planted facts in shorter documents

Given enough room to answer, it found 15 of 15 facts we had planted to see whether it could find them in a 16K-token window (a window is the most text it can take in at once), and 12 of 15 at 32K.

Its longest input fit in under 6 GiB of graphics memory

Its full 128,000-token window fit in under 6 GiB of observed graphics memory.

The mid-size download matched the top score

Of three official files, the mid-size 4-bit Q4_K_M matched the top score at ordinary size and speed.

What to watch for

Empty replies with no error

It reasons before it answers, and that reasoning is paid from the reply budget. When the budget runs out, the reply comes back with no error and no text. Short answers came back every time only at 1,024 tokens.

It found almost no planted facts in long documents and summarized wrongly

It found 1 of 15 hidden facts at 65K and 0 of 15 at its 128,000-token maximum. Asked to summarize the whole long document, it wrote a fluent summary that got things wrong.

No reply limit we tried gave a reliable 500-word answer

For an answer of about 500 words, no budget we tried up to 4,096 tokens was reliable.

We tested it on the day it came out, on one build of llama.cpp; other programs and files were not tested.

How should you set it up?

Give short prose answers at least 1,024 tokens of reply budget, and check whether a reply was cut off. Keep long inputs near 16K tokens. In llama.cpp, send the repetition penalty as repeat_penalty: the build we used silently ignores repetition_penalty, the name many clients use.
