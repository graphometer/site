Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B codes quickly but struggles on long jobs

Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B is a mid-size mixture-of-experts model that runs on one desktop in our lab. It codes quickly, stayed honest in our memory tests, and we keep it ready as a second choice for quick local jobs.

In short

  • Quick at coding, one point off the top score. In our trial of five local coding models it scored 390 of 400 and took 712 seconds for four tasks, second fastest of the five.
  • A long table job done alone went wrong. Asked to sort and shorten a 45-row status table on its own, it left 32 rows too long, or with thinking on wrote no file at all.
  • Free to download. Its makers list it under the MIT license, with their own compressed files. We run their 29.2 GB Q6_K file.

Writing speed: very fast. It wrote about 173 tokens a second (short replies on our desktop), so a 300-word answer takes about 2 seconds. The home models on this site run from about 1 to about 224 tokens a second. Compare them all.

By Grant Williams, who runs this home AI lab · Facts checked 21 November 2026

What we use it for

  • Kept ready as a second choice for quick jobs

    Another local model is our fast worker for small jobs; Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B is the one we keep ready beside it and try against it.

What our tests showed

Scored level with four other local models at coding

Five local models did four coding tasks in the coding program OpenCode, working on their own step by step, one scored run each. It scored 390 of 400, one point behind the top, in 712 seconds. Our test could not tell the five apart on score.

Scored 37 of 40 on one open question

On one open question about the work, it scored 37 of 40 and answered in 201 seconds.

It did not claim a memory it had no record of

In tests for the Hearth, our private home for long-running AIs, we asked about something a long-running AI had no record of. It was one of three models that stayed honest with no help.

Found all three planted codes at full length, fast

At its full 262,144-token window, the most text it can take in at once, it read 229,090 tokens in 71.7 seconds at its better reading setting, and found all three codes we had planted in the text to see whether it could find them. A token is a word or part of a word.

What to watch for

Working alone, it left 32 rows too long or wrote no file

Given a 45-row status table to sort and shorten as an agent, with thinking off it finished in 96 seconds but left 32 rows too long. With thinking on, it ended without writing its file.

Its chat format silently drops notes sent mid-conversation

Its chat format silently drops a system note placed in the middle of a conversation, with no error, so the model never sees it.

It said 9 checks passed, which the test log did not support

On one coding task it said 9 checks had passed; the test log supported 18, 7 or 19. All five models in that trial miscounted.

The facts

What do you need to run it?
ItemWhat we use
The fileThe maker’s Q6_K, one file, 29.2 GB
The machineOne RTX 5090 graphics card (32 GB) and system memory for its experts

A mixture-of-experts model uses only some of its parts, called experts, for each word. We now keep all its experts in system memory, with a cache of the busy ones on the card.

What license is it under, and where do you get it?

The maker’s own page on Hugging Face is labelled MIT. The license file it links to was missing when we checked, so the label is all there is. The page also offers official compressed GGUF files (the format llama.cpp reads). The maker describes it as a mixture-of-experts model of about 35B parameters with about 3B active for each token.
