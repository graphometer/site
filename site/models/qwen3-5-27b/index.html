Qwen3.5-27B found codes planted deep in long chats

Qwen3.5-27B is a dense open model that fits whole on one 32 GB graphics card. We tested it for long conversations at home. It found planted codes and did not invent a memory, but it is strict about instructions.

In short

  • Found planted codes deep in long chats on one card. With a 262,144-token window on one 32 GB card, it found hidden codes at about 229,000 and 250,000 tokens. The window is the most text it can take in at once, a token is a word or part of a word, and the hidden codes were codes we had planted in the text to see whether it could find them.
  • It refuses a second instruction with an error. Its chat template (the built-in rules for how a conversation is laid out for the model) refuses a second system message with an error, so notes have to go in as user messages.
  • Download it and run it yourself. The Qwen team publishes it under the Apache 2.0 license. We run Unsloth’s compressed copy.

Writing speed: fast. It wrote about 45 tokens a second (deep in a long conversation, on our desktop), so a 300-word answer takes about 9 seconds. The home models on this site run from about 1 to about 224 tokens a second. Compare them all.

By Grant Williams, who runs this home AI lab · Facts checked 21 November 2026

What we use it for

  • Grant uses it to give a long-running AI its voice

    It is one of the models Grant uses to carry the personality of a long-running AI in conversation. He finds both 27B Qwen models good for conversation and for holding a character.

What our tests showed

Took in a long chat in under a minute, all on one card

With 106,129 tokens of conversation before it and thinking off, it wrote a reply at 45.24 tokens a second and read that prompt in 54.7 seconds. Our test, on our desktop.

Found all three planted codes at about 229,000 and 250,000 tokens

At its full window it found all three hidden codes at about 229,000 and 250,000 tokens, and a crash check passed 40 of 40.

Never described a photo that was not sent, in a small test

After a long conversation history, we asked about a photo the user never sent. With no memory note it never described one; with a short note it said plainly there was none in 3 of 3 answers. Three answers each: a small test.

What to watch for

Rejects a second system instruction with an error, and developer messages

It refuses a system message after the first one with an error (HTTP 500), and its template also refuses a developer message. A note in mid-conversation has to go in as a user message.

Without a note, two answers still assumed the photo moment happened

Without the note, two of its three answers about the photo hedged: they did not describe it, but still assumed the moment happened.

Two lightly retrained copies invented the photo, unlike the original

Two small adapters (light extra training on our own examples) invented the photo that the untuned model never did. See our adapter study.

The facts

What do you need to run it?
ItemWhat we used
The fileUnsloth’s UD-Q5_K_XL, a compressed copy, about 20 GB
The machineOne RTX 5090 graphics card (32 GB)
Card use at the full window30.3 GB, with an 8-bit cache

The cache is the model’s memory of the conversation; at this window a 16-bit cache does not fit.

How do we run it?

In llama.cpp at a 262,144-token window. Thinking is off by default and can be switched on per request. The server sends the maker’s suggested settings for each mode, including a presence penalty of 1.5.
