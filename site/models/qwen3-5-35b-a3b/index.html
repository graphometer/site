Fast writing but many invented details

With thinking off, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B invented details in 22 of 36 replies. With thinking on, its private work before answering, all 10 replies held every required fact and one invented a detail. Check every reply before sending.

In short

  • It wrote fastest of the three models we tested. Its median, or middle, writing speed was 192 tokens a second. A token is a small piece of text. Qwen3.8-27B reached 62 and Hemmingway-1 reached 51. It stayed inside the length both requests asked for (story and newsletter) in all six tries.
  • It invented the most details of the three models we tested. Two Claude Sonnet AI models checked the replies. An invented detail is a specific fact, promise or detail the sender never gave. That happened in 22 of 36 replies with thinking off, against 16 for Qwen3.8-27B and seven for Hemmingway-1. Examples included refund timelines and a school name. All three bakery replies left [Customer Name] unfilled.
  • Two AI readers rarely chose it as most human of the three models we tested. The AI readers were Grok 4.7 and GLM 5.3. They ranked tries 1 and 2 of each request, making 24 sets of three unnamed answers. Each picked Qwen3.5 as most human-sounding in 2 of 24 sets, counting ties. No person read or ranked the replies.

Writing speed: very fast. It wrote about 192 tokens a second (short writing replies, thinking off, on our desktop), so a 300-word answer takes about 2 seconds. That time is worked out from the writing speed, not timed. The home models in our speed table run from about 1 to about 224 tokens a second. Compare them all.

By Grant Williams, who runs Graphometer · Facts checked 27 November 2026

What our tests showed

Thinking on included every required fact

All 10 replies held every required fact and one of 10 invented a detail. The median wait before the first visible word was 5.75 seconds. With thinking off it was 0.12 seconds across 36 replies.

Video-call replies used numbered steps

All three video-call replies used a numbered list. They also added introductions and tips, so the right format did not make them usable with no edits.

What to watch for

Some invented details appeared in replies judged usable

The two AI checkers found invented material in 22 of 36 replies with thinking off. They separately judged 22 of 36 usable with no edits. The matching counts are a coincidence. The groups overlap: 10 replies were marked both ways. A reply with a small invented detail could still be judged usable.

Extra material surrounded the requested piece

Five of 36 replies were marked as not being just the piece asked for. Two replies to a request for one message offered three options and tips. All three video-call replies added an introduction and a tip around the steps.

Thinking on still missed parts of the requests

Holding every required fact did not mean following the whole request. Six of 10 thinking-on replies missed some other part of it. Both credit replies offered options and advice instead of one message. One story opened a letter the request said must stay unopened. The other left out the missed bus the request asked for.

This covers short English pieces only. It did not test code or long documents. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is the newer version and has its own guide on this site.

The facts

What did we test?

We sent 12 everyday writing requests. They covered emails, personal messages, a reference letter, a bakery apology, a sentence rewrite, a condolence note, a story opening, video-call steps and a community-garden newsletter. Each request was tried three times with thinking off. This gave 36 replies for each model. Five requests were also tried twice with thinking on for Qwen3.5 and Hemmingway-1. This gave 10 replies for each.

Qwen3.8-27B was the third comparison model. All three ran one at a time on one RTX 5090 with 32 GB. They used the same llama.cpp build. There were no instructions beyond the request itself.

How did we check the replies?

The two AI checkers used a checklist fixed before the run. Model names were hidden behind codes. Placeholders such as [Customer Name] counted as not usable with no edits. A reply could contain every required fact and still miss another part of the request, such as tone, format or a story event. Whether a reply was usable as written depended on the checker. A stricter second check of Hemmingway-1’s replies found fewer usable, while its count of invented details held steady.

Which copy and settings did we use?

We ran unsloth’s compressed UD-Q5_K_XL copy. The file was 26.4 GB. The model has 35 billion internal values called parameters. A3B means about 3 billion of them are active for each token. Apache 2.0 is an open license the maker chose.

The maker’s settings were temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20 and presence penalty 1.5 with thinking off, changing to 1.0, 0.95, 20 and 1.5 with thinking on. Replies could use up to 2,048 tokens with thinking off and 16,384 with it on. None reached either limit.
