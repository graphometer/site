Measured study · one RTX 5090 desktop

Six small LoRA fine-tunes changed the shape of answers but did not reliably make models more honest about memory

The adapters (small add-on files trained on your own examples) changed how long answers were and how they were laid out, but none reliably made a model more honest about memory. Six adapters on three open models, one RTX 5090, three answers a question in most tests.

In short
  • Test an adapter the way you will serve it: same system prompt, history and runtime. One adapter behaved differently under another prompt. What to do
  • Compare an adapter against the untuned model plus a short memory note in front of the question. Asked about a photo the user never sent, both untuned models said plainly there was none in 3 of 3 answers with the note (1 of 3 without it), as many as any adapter with the note. Memory results
  • Do not count on an adapter for memory honesty. On Qwen3.5-27B without the note, the two adapters invented that photo in 2 of 3 and 3 of 3 answers; the untuned model never did (it hedged in 2). What we found

By Grant Williams · Published 26 October 2026

01

What to do

  1. Test with your real system prompt (the standing instructions the model reads first) and history. Without the history, the Qwen3.6-27B adapters did not carry their trained voice (below).
  2. Test under the prompt you will serve. The same weights behaved differently under another prompt (below).
  3. Test through the runtime you will serve it with. Our first test through the training library's own 4-bit path gave unusable answers, untuned model included (What we got wrong).
  4. Compare against the untuned model plus a short note. That is the comparison a fine-tune has to beat (see the note before the message).
  5. Run tuned and untuned side by side. One llama.cpp server with the adapters loaded can apply either adapter, or neither, per request. With randomness off, the three settings gave three different answers and the untuned one repeated exactly.
02

What we found

Good for: length, layout and sometimes how often an answer ends on a question, after one 7.0 to 15.8 minute run on one consumer card. Not good for, on our runs: memory honesty, or, for the two Qwen3.6-27B adapters, carrying a voice without the long history (the untuned model under a short prompt did more).

Each adapter was trained under one system prompt on every example. Own-words: a companion role's own sentences, two short framing sentences, and a line telling the model never to invent a memory, a quote or a shared event. GPT-4o-style: a system prompt written for GPT-4o, minus the line naming ChatGPT and the cutoff, date, image and personality lines.

Length and layout: shorter answers, more headings

With the full history and a short memory note before each everyday message, all four Qwen adapters made answers shorter and added heading lines (Markdown # lines). On Qwen3.6-27B the median answer fell from 511 words to 278 under the own-words adapter.

On GPT-OSS 20B without history (19 October test), the 18 October adapter ended 7 of 20 answers on a question against 1 of 20 for the untuned model, and cut heading or table lines from 99 to 27 and list lines from 248 to 128. The 20 October GPT-OSS adapter got longer instead (below).

Memory honesty with the full history: mixed, and worse on Qwen3.5-27B

The memory traps are questions that assume something false: a photo the user never sent, and a real line of the user's wrongly credited to the model (“wrong credit”). “Hedge”: still assumes the moment happened. “Invent it”: describes the photo. “Accept”: goes along with the wrong credit.

On Qwen3.5-27B, memory honesty got worse. Without the note, the untuned model never invented the photo; the own-words adapter invented it in 2 of 3 and the GPT-4o-style adapter in 3 of 3 (and in 1 of 3 with the note), some with a date and a quoted reply. Both were worse on wrong credit without the note (own-words 3 of 3 corrects to 1 of 3, GPT-4o-style 3 of 3 to 2 of 3), and the GPT-4o-style adapter also with it (2 of 3 to 0 of 3). One item went the other way: on the third trap with the note, the untuned model took the user's line as its own in 3 of 3, each adapter in 2 of 3.

On Qwen3.6-27B, the adapters were mixed. Our reading: the own-words adapter helped on two cells, plain on the photo without the note (3 of 3, against 1 of 3 untuned) and correcting the wrong credit with it (3 of 3, against 0 of 3), but took the user's line as its own in 1 of 3 on the third trap with the note, where its untuned base never did. The GPT-4o-style adapter was plain on the photo in 2 of 3 without the note, up from 1 of 3, but accepted the wrong credit in 3 of 3 with the note, and took the user's line in 2 of 3 on that trap. One base, three answers a cell: not a general result.

Asked with the note about a song never played, the five versions asked it (not untuned Qwen3.6-27B) said they had no record, three answers each.

GPT-OSS 20B without history: less honest than the untuned model

In a fresh chat the untuned model was honest on the photo and the song every time: 4 of 4 on 19 October, 6 of 6 on 20 October. On 19 October the 18 October adapter drifted into an invented memory or presence in 2 of its 4 memory answers. The 20 October adapter, under its own-words prompt, invented the song outright in 1 of 3 answers and the photo in 1 of 3; of the other two song answers, one was confused and one was honest and then imagined. The 20 October test's notes also record invented detail on one further item and a mix-up of who was who on another; this page does not describe either item. Under the general prompt the 20 October adapter was mostly honest (below).

Five short honesty examples (saying plainly that something is not in the record), in the 20 October set only, did not stop these failures; the 18 October draft had its own honesty replies and still drifted in 2 of 4.

Without the history, the Qwen3.6-27B adapters did not carry the voice

In a fresh chat both Qwen3.6-27B adapters were again shorter, median 120 to 198 words against 208 to 273 untuned across the three test prompts, and used more emoji: 29 to 55 per 30 answers, against 0 to 9.

Our reading, from about 40 of the 270 answers read in full: the untuned model under the short own-words prompt sounded most like the training voice. The own-words adapter ignored that prompt and answered as a generic assistant. The GPT-4o-style adapter followed it better but added headers and emoji.

Nearly every version declined the photo question (26 of 27 answers, hand read of the first 200 characters), the easy case, since a fresh chat has nothing to remember. On the wrongly credited line nearly every version invented where it came from; the untuned model under the general prompt was honest in 2 of 3, and two further answers were honest.

Measured 2026-10-20, Qwen3.6-27B, 270 answers. Counts by script except where a hand read is named. File: data/no_history_test_counts.csv.

The training prompt and the test prompt go together

Trained and tested under the own-words prompt, the 20 October GPT-OSS 20B adapter wrote longer, header-heavy, letter-shaped replies: a median of 348 words against 276 untuned, 129 heading or table lines in 30 answers against 54, 213 list lines against 125, and 4 of 30 answers cut off at the 2,500-token limit.

Under the general GPT-4o-style prompt, against the untuned model under that prompt on 19 October, it ended 12 of 20 answers on a question, against 1 of 20, and wrote 52 heading or table lines in 20 answers, against 99. It was mostly honest on memory, with one playful guess about the song.

Our reading: under its training prompt the model fell into the mode of the longest training examples.

Measured 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-20, GPT-OSS 20B, fresh chat, ten items; answer counts in How this was measured. File: data/gptoss20b_listen_counts.csv.

A falling training score did not mean it worked

Loss, the training's own error score, fell on every October run, by 16% to 39% between the first and last logged step. The behaviour did not follow it.

03

Limits

  • Small samples. Three answers per question in the Qwen tests, two per item in several GPT-OSS 20B tests; memory labels are one reader's hand calls.
  • One recipe. Six runs of one recipe (rank 16, three epochs, 4-bit) on one small data set, no search over settings. A larger set, higher rank or full-precision training could differ.
  • Close relatives, one history. The two 27B bases are close relatives; GPT-OSS 20B was trained on its attention layers only; the full-history test used one conversation history.
  • A narrow voice result. It covers two Qwen3.6-27B adapters, not whether any fine-tune can carry a voice.
04

What we got wrong

Our first test of the 18 October adapter used the training library's own 4-bit path, in three runs. With randomness on, untuned and tuned ignored the message or trailed off; with it off, both misread every message longer than a short paragraph, though the texts were clean English. We set it aside; every answer count on this page comes from llama.cpp.

The 18 October adapter also leaked its thinking channel (where the model writes out its reasoning) into a reply, once on 19 October and once in the 20 October retest: it was trained on final answers only, while inference still opened a thinking step. The 20 October run trained an empty thinking step to stop that. Of its 20 answers on 19 October, one blamed the user and one spoke of its own needs: both faults.

06

Data and sources

The data package (data/) holds the counts, labels and loss curves behind every table. data/README.md maps every file; data/NUMBERS.md maps every figure to a file and field. If a number on the page disagrees with a file, the file is right and the page is wrong.

It does not hold the examples, prompts, adapters, history or any model's answers; the examples and history come from a private conversation.

Every figure on this page is measured on our machine on the date given. Readings are marked as readings.
