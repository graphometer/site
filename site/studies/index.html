Running AI models at home

01
Reference · 27 September to 25 October 2026 · one RTX 5090 desktop

Local AI models on one RTX 5090 desktop, October 2026

Thirteen models added to one RTX 5090 desktop in a month, six re-measured, three retired. A thread-pinning bundle made 13 of 15 clean test pairs about 7 to 81 percent faster, and two got slower. The bundle: cores 0 to 7 with taskset, 8 threads, and the OpenMP spin setting GOMP_SPINCOUNT=10000000; the spin setting was not run on its own. 27 September to 25 October 2026.

Measured study · 20 September 2026 · one RTX 5090 desktop

Six Qwen vision models in Ollama, two also on the CPU

Six Qwen vision models through Ollama on one RTX 5090 desktop. On the card, Qwen3-VL 30B-A3B found all 39 known strings at 1.3 to 3.2 seconds a warm picture. On the CPU, the server ran 4 of 24 threads unless told otherwise, and its first 4K picture took 273 seconds; 16 threads took 111, and a 1,536-pixel cap brought it to 38.5, at a cost on small text.

Guide · arithmetic with measured anchors · runs of 15 September 2026

Estimating Inkling 975B's speed from bytes per token

Count the routed expert bytes in the actual quantization, then divide an effective expert throughput by that budget. Recorded short-prompt prose on this desktop implies an effective expert throughput of 31.0 to 34.3 GB/s, an arithmetic proxy rather than a direct RAM measurement. Inkling 975B's arithmetic budget is 6.01 GB of routed expert weights per token; its short-prose sample had a server-reported decode rate of 3.43 tokens per second alone; a different short-prose sample on the pair decoded at 3.13.

What reasoning effort changes on Qwen3.8-27B

10
Measured study · 1 and 2 November 2026 · one RTX 5090 desktop · 19 mixture-of-experts models

Two llama.cpp settings for MoE models bigger than your GPU

With nothing else changed, --load-mode dio read a 20,000-token prompt 1.60 to 3.13 times faster on 14 memory-mapped models and cut cold starts to 29 to 63 percent of the time on all 16 tested, while writing speed moved by 3.4 percent or less; free RAM fell to as little as 5.1 GiB. Setups that added the expert cache (--moe-cache-mib) wrote short replies 1.44 to 2.88 times faster on 11 large models, alongside other changes on most of them. The costs sit beside the gains: a nearly full card, 13 setups that did not run, and cached output that differs from before. Exact flags, builds and logs.

AI agents and coding tools

Letting agents say no, and how coding agents and local models did on real jobs.

20
Proposal · Agency Layer Pilot I, 30 August to 3 September 2026 · study paused

Kindness doesn't cost

Give AI agents a way to ask, object, pause, decline or step away, and honor it. In our pilot, completion stayed high on ten ordinary scripted tasks. This small check does not prove the moves have no cost. When a task had something wrong with it, an AI scorer marked 127 of 240 runs with a paragraph of permission as speaking up before acting, against 76 of 240 runs with no permission. The scorer failed its validation and made different errors in different conditions, so these counts do not establish how big the real effect is, or reliably which way it goes. The small protocol and the paragraph are on the page. What the pilot could and could not show is there too, with its data.

Build report · 12 to 17 October 2026 · one ASUS ROG Flow Z13 laptop · Letta Code 0.34.10

Running Letta Code locally: what it reached for, what stopped it

At factory settings and never signed in, one agent turn of Letta Code 0.34.10 looked up api.letta.com and the npm registry and made 17 connection attempts to port 443. With six environment switches set together, the same trace showed none. The open-source window app links Google Fonts, and Chromium inside it downloaded a spell-check dictionary from Google; our fork removes both. The sealed setup runs in a bubblewrap sandbox with no network and one socket door to the local model. Two audits found a saved API key usable by any program in the sealed sandbox (fixed, tested with stand-ins) and agents able to read each other's files in one setup (open, found in the code). Exact switches, sandbox command, checks, costs and limits.

Counted piece · 353 scripted conversations per snapshot, run 4 July 2026 · text surface only

Three GPT-4o snapshots, counted against each other

The May, August and November 2024 snapshots answered the same 354 scripted conversations on 4 July 2026; 353 are used, 1,678 replies each, after one matched cut. November puts an em dash in 86% of its replies (1,446 of 1,678) against 17% and 19%, and asks 2.55 questions per reply against 1.07 and 0.99. August writes the shortest replies, 77 words against 122 and 155. May uses the most lists and bold type. On a short-chat set with real repeats, November sits apart from both others on length, on em dashes per reply and on exclamation marks; on the share of replies with an em dash its gap sits outside the run-to-run gap against May and inside it against August. A data package and a 36-pair sample pack ship with it.

Measured study · adapters trained 18 and 20 October 2026 · GPT-OSS 20B, Qwen3.5-27B, Qwen3.6-27B

What a small LoRA fine-tune does and does not do, measured

Six small adapters on GPT-OSS 20B, Qwen3.5-27B and Qwen3.6-27B, each trained on a small curated set. Qwen3.6-27B was tested with and without a 166-message history. Qwen3.5-27B was tested with that history only. GPT-OSS 20B was tested in a fresh chat only. With the history and a short memory note before each everyday message, Qwen3.6-27B's median answer fell from 511 to 278 words under one adapter. On Qwen3.5-27B, with no memory note, both adapters invented a photo the untuned model never did, though two of the untuned model's three answers hedged: they still assumed the moment happened. With the note, one adapter still invented it in 1 of 3. On Qwen3.6-27B, with no history, a short prompt on the untuned model did more for the voice than either of its adapters.
